What Is an RNG?

A Random Number Generator (RNG) is a computational algorithm that produces a continuous stream of random numbers — thousands per second. When you press spin, the game uses the RNG's current output to determine the exact position of every symbol on the grid. The result is mathematically independent of every spin that came before it.

Online slots generally use a pseudo-random number generator to select outcomes. This page does not claim a specific Fury of Anubis algorithm, testing laboratory or audit schedule without a game-specific primary document. The practical player check is the operator’s regulatory status and the information displayed inside the game.

Why Does It Feel Rigged?

The feeling of a rigged game almost always comes down to one of three things:

✗
Misunderstanding variance

Long losing runs can occur in random games. Pragmatic Play does not publish an exact natural bonus frequency on its public page, so a bonus is never “due” after a claimed number of spins.

✗
Confirmation bias

We remember the 50-spin bonus drought vividly. We barely notice the 3-spin trigger that occasionally happens. The RNG doesn't care about your session history.

✗
Short-session RTP misconceptions

96.52% RTP is a long-run theoretical figure across billions of spins. In a 200-spin session you might return 30% or 400% — that's variance, not manipulation.

The Bottom Line

The defensible conclusion is narrower than “trust every copy of the game.” Pragmatic Play publishes Fury of Anubis and its headline specifications. Players should confirm the operator, jurisdiction, displayed rules and RTP before risking money.

If anything looks inconsistent, stop playing and contact the operator or relevant regulator. Session tracking can help you control spending, but a personal sample is not large enough to certify an RNG or guarantee convergence to the published RTP.
